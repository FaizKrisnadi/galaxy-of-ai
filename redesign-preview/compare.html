Will AI improve work?

A faster task and a changing labour market are different things. These two studies help us look at both.

An experiment · 2023

Writing tasks

Noy and Zhang, Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence.

What they studied
453 college-educated professionals completed incentivised writing tasks. Half were assigned access to ChatGPT.
What they found
Average completion time fell and output quality rose in the experiment.
What it cannot tell us
Short writing tasks do not show what happens to employment, wages or every kind of work.

A labour-market study · revised 2026

Employment patterns

Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen, Canaries in the Coal Mine?

What they studied
US payroll data, comparing employment patterns across occupations and age groups by AI exposure.
What they found
In the revised study, employment for younger workers in more AI-exposed occupations lagged behind comparable less-exposed workers. The authors found no evidence of economy-wide displacement.
What it cannot tell us
The authors describe these patterns as early indicators, not causal estimates of AI’s effect. Results concern their US data and period.
Read the study ↗

These findings are compatible: AI may help people complete particular tasks faster while employment outcomes vary by occupation and age. The studies do not, on their own, settle the wider question.
