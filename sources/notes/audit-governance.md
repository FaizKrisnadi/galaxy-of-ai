# Governance abstract audit — 2 November 2026

This batch checked the abstract or equivalent original-author publication summary for the 11 specified catalogue IDs. The longer articles, methods, data and claims were not independently checked. Findings below are attributed to each author's abstract; they are not independent confirmation. Source availability is the state of the web tool on this date. The catalogue and graph were not edited.

- **geith-regime-complex** — Abstract read in the [authors’ accepted manuscript](https://magnuslundgren.org/papers/Geith%20et%20al.%202025%20GSQ.pdf), an earlier version of the [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/isagsq/ksag145). The authors examine 119 international institutions in AI governance between 2014 and 2024 and report low hierarchy, low functional differentiation, and European concentration. Their explanation combines power, function and institutional path dependence. The accepted manuscript may differ from the published version; body and underlying dataset not read in this audit.
- **kim-regime-complex-documents** — Abstract read in the [author’s Research Square preprint](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-9374171/v1/4093925c-21ac-4518-af8e-92795e899f68.pdf?c=1779171999), a version dated May 2026 of the [catalogued journal article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11077-026-09632-w). Kim frames global AI governance as overlapping control, rights and safety regimes and argues coordination should be tailored to each. Preprint and journal version may differ; body and dataset unread.
- **patro-santacreu-targeted-trade** — [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis article abstract and introduction](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/review/2026/sep/geopolitical-distance-and-targeted-trade) read. Using product-level export-intervention data, the authors report interventions concentrated in high-technology products and high-value trade; they describe a nonlinear relationship with geopolitical distance. The detailed analysis was not read; association does not itself establish causation.
- **lundgren-tallberg-preferences** — Abstract read at the [authors’ arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14585), an alternative to the inaccessible catalogue SSRN URL. The authors report a seven-country conjoint survey experiment in which respondents tended to prefer safety, public governance and international coordination over the alternatives. This is the authors’ account of surveyed choices, not proof of a universal public preference; methods and country-level results unread in this audit.
- **oecd-wp93-gov-genai** — [OECD publication-page abstract](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/generative-ai-experimentation-in-government_42815683-en.html) read. The OECD describes a review of guidelines from 14 countries, literature and cases, reporting fragmented practices and weak measurement and proposing an evaluation framework. These are abstract-level claims; the 68-page paper was not read. The abstract was accessible via this web tool even though previous project attempts could not retrieve it.
- **culot-ai-procurement** — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jbl.70088) read. Based on 38 expert interviews and a follow-up questionnaire, the authors identify tensions involving public value, data stewardship and accountable decisions in AI-enabled procurement. The full article and study detail were not assessed. The abstract is now accessible via this web tool despite a previous recorded 403.
- **pursiainen-critical-infrastructure** — [Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41125-026-00115-w) read. The authors analyse EU documents on critical-infrastructure resilience and AI across regulation, research policy and standardisation, concluding that change has been incremental and uneven. This is the abstract’s interpretation; the body was not read here. The page was accessible through this tool despite previous cookie-gate failures.
- **restrepo-firm-compliance** — Abstract read at [Centre Perelman’s author-affiliated summary](https://centreperelman.be/compliance-under-the-eu-ai-act-how-firms-navigate-regulatory-complexity-in-the-digital-landscape/), corresponding to the [catalogued journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clsr.2026.106390), whose DOI redirected to an inaccessible publisher page. The authors describe exploratory questionnaires and interviews with professionals from 14 European firms and suggest a partial, uneven move toward more coordinated compliance. This is an exploratory sample and does not represent all firms; full article unread.
- **anastasopoulos-discretion** — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/puar.70201) read. The author argues that replacing administrative discretion with algorithms can threaten accountability, equity, individual consideration and reason-giving, and proposes AI support for human officials. This is an author’s normative argument, not an experimentally verified general effect; the full article was not assessed. Abstract accessible through this tool despite an earlier 403.
- **van-es-deliberation** — [Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44206-026-00294-y) read. The authors present D4M, a data and AI ethics deliberation tool co-created with a media company, and describe iterative prototypes and evaluative focus groups. The abstract does not establish its effectiveness across organisations; article body unread.
- **ustenko-participation** — [Frontiers abstract](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/political-science/articles/10.3389/fpos.2026.1842794/full) read. The authors propose a stage-specific framework for AI-assisted multilingual participation and illustrate parts of it with a Civil 20 India 2023 survey spanning 5,152 participants, 64 countries and 24 languages. The abstract says two later stages remain theoretical, and observed response patterns do not establish that an AI tool improved participation. Full methods and underlying data unread.
