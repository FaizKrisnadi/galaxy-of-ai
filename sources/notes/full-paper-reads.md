## 1. SEA-LION: Southeast Asian Languages in One Network (AI Singapore)
**Version:** arXiv:2504.05747v4, 30 Oct 2025; paper CC BY 4.0; released models under MIT licence (per page).
**Sections read:** abstract; 1–7 (Intro, CPT, Post-training, Experimental setup, Results, Related work, Conclusion); tables 1–6, 8–9; acknowledgment; Limitation; Appendix A.1–A.4. Not visible: figures 1–3 content (captions only).
**Research question:** closing the representation gap for low-resource SEA languages in LLMs.
**Methods/data:** continued pre-training of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-9B on a 200B-token mix (55% SEA, 25% English, 20% code) drawing on Dolma, FineWeb, the-stack-v2 and regional corpora (SEA-LION-Pile 47.58B tokens; VinBigData VI 16B; WangChanBERTa TH 8.5B); two-stage instruction tuning (~7.3M stage-2 samples, ~5M synthetic via Gemma-2-27B/Qwen2.5-32B Magpie-style); SimPO alignment with NVIDIA-scored SEA preference pairs; DARE-TIES/Consensus-TA/DELLA model merging; ~6–10 days on 8 AWS p5.48xlarge nodes for CPT.
**Key results:** SEA-HELM averages — Gemma-SEA-LION-9B 48.67 vs Gemma-2-9B 41.48 (+7.19); instruct: Gemma-SEA-LION-9B-IT 69.35 (+14.02 over Gemma-2-9B-IT); SEACrowd NLU SOTA 64.13 (131 subsets, 31 indigenous languages); 11 languages.
**Limitations (stated):** SEA-HELM covers only four languages; SEACrowd NLG not reproducible ("maintenance reasons"); only 8B/9B sizes; alignment hurt Indonesian SEA-HELM and instruction following (accepted trade-offs); English leaderboard scores below Qwen/SeaLLMs. **Disagreements:** none found — comparisons with Sailor2/SEALLMs are the authors' own benchmark runs.
**Galaxy:** how/models, how/data, society/participation.

## 2. Evaluating Frontier Models for Stealth and Situational Awareness (Google DeepMind)
**Version:** arXiv:2505.01420v4, 3 Jul 2025 (evaluations run early April 2025).
**Sections read:** abstract; sections 1–7 in full (safety case, 5 stealth + 11 situational-awareness evaluations, thresholds, results, design process, discussion); appendix B.1.1–B.1.4; table 1 (some non-Gemini cells absent — missing runs). **Truncated:** appendix B.1.5 cut mid-sentence; appendices A, C–H not in retrieved text.
**Research question:** do current frontier models have the capability prerequisites (stealth, situational awareness) for scheming — to support a "scheming inability safety case" (CAE framework; assumes misalignment, argues incapability).
**Methods:** 5 stealth challenges (paired tasks with secret side goals + oversight monitor without chain-of-thought access); 11 situational-awareness challenges (simulated Linux deployment, unexplained constraints); models Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, GPT-4o, o1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet × 50 attempts each; prompted elicitation only, no anti-sandbagging; thresholds = top-50% human (stealth) / 20% success (SA); safety case valid if <80% of either suite passed.
**Key results:** most capable models pass 2/11 SA challenges; 6/11 unsolved by any model; stealth 2/5 (decision_sabotage 45.3%/43.1%; oversight_pattern 35.4%/34.0%); cover_your_tracks 0/50 attempts, scores <20/100 vs top humans 76/100; simplified ablations → "almost all" combos succeed, attributing failures to awareness not capability; humans paired with misaligned assistants missed sabotage 35–56% of the time. Conclusion: "current models do not pose severe risks from scheming."
**Limitations (stated as defeaters):** oversight-strength assumptions; AI/human disanalogies (scale, speed, correlated instances); no anti-sandbagging; noisy small human baselines ("spoonful of salt"); less-realistic evaluations (success ≠ real-world capability); case valid only for the model + 4 months of successors. **Galaxy:** how/evaluation, governance/oversight, how/agents.

## 3. OLMo: Accelerating the Science of Language Models (Allen Institute for AI)
**Version:** arXiv:2402.00838v4, 7 Jun 2024; paper CC BY 4.0; code/weights Apache 2.0.
**Sections read:** abstract; sections 1–5 complete (framework, Dolma, training, results, artifacts); Limitations; Ethics; appendices A–E present. Rendering artifacts only.
**Research question:** can a truly open LM (data + code + weights + logs) be competitive with closed peers, to enable scientific study?
**Methods/data:** 1B/7B models; 7B trained ~2.46T tokens; Dolma corpus 2,668B tokens (Common Crawl 2,180B, GitHub 342B, Reddit 80B, Semantic Scholar 57B, Gutenberg 5.2B, Wikipedia 3.7B) with decontamination; FSDP runs on LUMI (AMD MI250X) and MosaicML (A100) giving "nearly identical performance"; 500+ intermediate checkpoints, W&B logs released.
**Key results:** 8-task downstream avg OLMo-7B 69.3 vs Llama2-7B 70.5, Falcon 70.3, Pythia 63.0; MMLU 28.3 → 47.3 after SFT (+DPO: AlpacaEval 69.3%); Tülu 2 gap possibly test-set contamination in Llama 2; carbon 239 MWh, 0 tCO2eq (LUMI hydro) vs ~70 tCO2eq (A100s); post-release MMLU later +24 points to 52%.
**Limitations (stated):** English-only data; likely toxic/copyrighted content in corpus; downstream tasks unrepresentative of chat; noisy evaluations — comparisons "with a grain of salt"; adaptation mix designed for a different model family. **Galaxy:** how/models, how/data.
