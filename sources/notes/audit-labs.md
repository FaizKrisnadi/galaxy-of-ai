# Reading audit: Mila, CHAI and Ada catalogue entries

Accessed 2 November 2026. This is a record-by-record first pass. Except where specified, **only the abstract, executive-summary or landing-page overview was read**. Descriptions of empirical findings are authors' claims, not independent replication; the methods, full results and limitations remain unread. For long reports, an executive-summary read does not mean the underlying report was read. These notes do not change the catalogue's recorded statuses.

## Mila records

- **mila-ecuas** — ECUAS metrics. The [catalogued OpenReview page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=O9u4lItD9K) returned a verification challenge; not bypassed. An [arXiv paper with the same title](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20490) has an accessible abstract, which proposes scoring rules to evaluate systems that supply predictions together with uncertainty, allowing different costs for errors and uncertain predictions. **Identity link provisional**: the challenged OpenReview page could not be inspected to match authors or versions, so do not merge as a verified same work yet. Abstract only, methods/results unread.
- **mila-gitchameleon** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12367), v2. The authors introduce 328 executable Python completion problems to test code generation against specific library versions; they report tested systems struggle with version-sensitive tasks. Abstract only; benchmark and results not independently examined. A separate existing audit note covers the same abstract.
- **mila-risk-rl** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03778), v2. Proposes a formulation and algorithms for risk-sensitive sequential decisions using conditional value-at-risk, with theoretical guarantees and claimed empirical trade-offs. Abstract only; proofs and experiments unread.
- **mila-sft-rotation** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10973). Proposes a fine-tuning constraint intended to preserve generalization while adapting to a task; authors report improved trade-offs on tested math-reasoning models. Abstract only; comparisons and generalizability unverified.
- **mila-diffusion-policy** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00295), v3. Proposes learning the order in which a masked diffusion model reveals sequence tokens, instead of using a fixed random or heuristic order. Abstract only; detailed evaluation unread.
- **mila-speech-grounding** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19468). Proposes training audio-language models to tie reasoning to timestamped audio segments; the authors report improvements on four speech benchmarks. Abstract only; benchmark designs and limitations unread.

## CHAI records

- **chai-tasra** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06924), v2. The paper proposes a taxonomy of societal-scale AI risks organised by accountability, whether actors are coordinated, and intent; hypothetical stories illustrate categories. Scenarios are not evidence of their likelihood. Abstract only; full framework unread.
- **chai-control-roadmap** — [IEEE Control Systems Society overview](https://ieeecss.org/control-societal-scale-challenges-road-map-2030), introduction and contents. A multidisciplinary control-systems roadmap identifies areas where control methods might address societal challenges, including infrastructure, healthcare, AI and safety. This is a field-wide roadmap, not exclusively an AI-risk paper. Landing-page overview only; the linked full report remains unread.
- **chai-teacher-selection-rlhf** — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15288), v3, currently titled *Active teacher selection for reward learning*. The authors model feedback providers with different expertise, rationality and costs, and report proof-of-concept applications to paper recommendations and vaccine testing. The catalogue uses an earlier title. Abstract only; methods and full outcomes unread.

## Ada Lovelace Institute records

- **ada-minding-business** — [briefing key insights](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/policy-briefing/minding-your-business/). The author examines how existing EU digital laws may address advanced AI assistants' effects on user autonomy, democracy and market competition, and recommends clearer application and enforcement. These are the author's legal analysis and proposals; they are not an independent finding about current legal obligations. Key insights only; full legal analysis unread.
- **ada-uk-regulation-options** — [feature overview](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/feature/ai-regulation-options-uk/). A September 2026 explainer compares four possible UK approaches, from relying on existing sector regulators to a comprehensive AI bill. These are scenarios at publication, not a current legal-status update. Overview and list of scenarios read; full analysis unread.
- **ada-care-consideration** — [executive summary and key findings](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/report/care-and-consideration/). A collaboration with NICE examines governance of AI for internal public-sector processes; it identifies provider dependence, information quality and accountability among concerns. It explicitly does not address tools for direct clinical use. Findings are reported by the authors; full methods and report unread.
- **ada-navigating-future** — [executive summary](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/report/navigating-the-future/). A UK landscape review examines AI in career guidance for young people, including opportunities and risks around access, accuracy and safeguarding. Its summary says the review engaged 36 stakeholders from 22 organisations and considered literature; this is not a representative survey. Full methodology and report unread.
