# Galaxy of AI — research guides

Ten reading guides plus one cross-area connections file, written 4 November 2026 from the Galaxy of AI library (372 records, 38 topics). They are self-contained essays: each answers one question using sources in the library, states what was actually read of each source, and keeps measured outcomes, forecasts, company claims and author arguments visibly apart.

## How to read the guides

Every guide has the same shape: a short answer, a source-anchored explanation, where researchers disagree, what remains uncertain, a starting reading list with direct links, the Galaxy topics it touches, and honest notes on the sources. The "Notes on the sources" section records what portion of each source was actually read for the guide (abstract, executive summary, specific pages, or full text) and anything that was inaccessible. Nothing is cited beyond what was read this session or verified in the library's own records — and where the two differ, the guide says so.

## How the guides were made

Each guide was written from the library's catalogue records (sources/catalogue.json) and verified against the sources themselves: either the library's page-anchored text extractions in sources/pdfs/ or a fresh read of the live page. Per-writer reading logs are in [logs/](logs/) (writer-A through writer-E), and the shared writing rules are in [logs/WRITING-RULES.md](logs/WRITING-RULES.md). Known standing corrections from the library's audits (ILO exposure ≠ job loss; Canaries = associations; CMA shares are UK+EEA ranges; IMF patent window predates generative AI; IEA figures are scenarios) are carried inside the guides.

## Known gaps

- The library's copyright topic holds exactly one source (the US Copyright Office report), so guide 9 describes the fight but not its outcome; EU text-and-data-mining law and the licensing market are missing.
- society/attitudes has no full-read record; guide 10 leans on Pew's report page and HAI's chapter summary rather than full studies.
- China, the US and the EU lack comprehensive official AI documents in the library, so guide 7's portraits of them lean on think-tank and firm analysis.
- Guide 5's METR result covers one population; the guide says so, but any single-study result should be re-checked before being quoted further.
- Two sources cited from library records could not be re-verified this session: Restrepo et al. (ScienceDirect bot wall) and parts of the SemiAnalysis article (paywall). Flagged in the relevant guides' notes.
